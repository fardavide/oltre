One — the world row
Two readings that point in opposite directions
A row that says BLOCKED and OPPORTUNITY in one breath reads as the app arguing with itself, and the reason is structural: both are set as verdicts, in the same voice, competing for the same slot. All three treatments below fix that the same way — one badge-shaped state per row, never two — and differ only on which reading gets it.
One change is shared by all three and is not optional: danger is split where it is generated. The distance band is astronomy, it is the same for all fifteen slots of a system, and it is known before anything is surveyed — so it is stated once, under the system header. Hazards are per-world and need a survey, so they sit on the row as words that carry their own arithmetic. No row ever prints a sum whose two halves have different scopes and different epistemic standing.

Two — the dispatch sheet
Raised from a world row. Three controls, one figure.
Bring back, send, home in — in that order, because it is the order of decreasing permanence: what your colony is short of changes over days, how many hulls you have changes over hours, and how long you will be away changes every check-in. The figure sits under a rule, above the verb, and is the only thing on the sheet that moves when you touch a control. Below it, the three lines that explain it, and nothing else: legs, danger, frontier.
The sheet has no cost line and no affordability state, and that is not an omission — §1 charges nothing per run. "Cannot afford" belongs to the Shipyard, and it is drawn there.
