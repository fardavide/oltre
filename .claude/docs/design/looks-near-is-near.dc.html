0.11.3 · the galaxy at three scales · design round trip
Looks near, is near
The galaxy is one-dimensional and the drawing has to agree, so the whole design is one move: fold the line into ten bands and let each band be a region. Path order stays index order, so a distance on the drawing is a distance in the game. The second dimension is bought for exactly one thing, which is the thing 0.11.0 measured and could not afford — ten region names, all legible at once. Everything else follows: a region is a place because it is a labelled stretch you can point at, the probe's hour ruler becomes four tick marks laid along the path instead of a component under it, and the whole galaxy fits on one 393dp screen with no scroll, no pan and no pinch.
Frames are seed 20260807, day 21, one probe out. Home is Calianova, 3:165, in Almiaren Deep, the seventh of ten regions. Star classes are drawn from GalaxyBalance.starMix as committed — DEEP 60/30/10, SETTLED 20/60/20, BURNING 10/30/60 — which is not the bias table RegionStrip.dc.html shipped with, so a few 0.11.0 details move: Calianova is standard with six worlds here, not dim with five. Clocks are read out of SurveyBalance and FleetBalance at main rather than assumed. Frames exclude the status bar and home indicator.
1 · The galaxy — ten bands, folded, one region each.
A serpentine whose path order is index order. Twenty-five stars a band at a 14dp pitch, a drawn turn at each end so the ten read as one folded line rather than a grid, and a deterministic perpendicular drift so it reads as sky rather than as a table. Class is size, brightness and the portrait ramp’s cold-violet lean, which makes each region's bias visible as texture: Nerimira Deep is 16 dim to 1 bright, Vokith Blaze is 2 to 16.
2 · The universe — one gesture up, not a screen.
Four galaxies swap into the same frame from the header chip; nothing pushes and there is nothing to come back from. Each disc is the same drawing at 1/5 scale — real texture, because star class is free — carrying the one fact that is true today: what it costs to get there. G2 and G4 are a 4h 40m probe; G1 is 8h 50m. Empires later tint a disc; they do not need a new surface.
3 · Navigation — two stops and one push. No free zoom.
Universe and galaxy are two states of one surface. Galaxy to system is a real push, because a system is a different kind of object and it is where you act. In between there is no zoom and no pan: a tap snaps the selection to the nearest star and a drag scrubs it, so 250 targets smaller than a fingertip cost nothing to miss. The caption is the 44dp target.
4 · The ledger — the filters and the sort go. Search stays.
Your reading of why they are useless is right and there is a floor under it: the ledger's rows are worlds and the outbound question is about systems. A probe is aimed at a star. So the ledger could not answer "where next" filtered, sorted or neither — wrong unit, before you reach the controls. It keeps what a list is for: finding a world you already know, by name.
5 · The tab lands on the map — and I am overruling myself.
0.11.0 argued the ledger, on the grounds that "five honest rows beat 250 dots of which one is yours". That argument was against a dot field and it still defeats one. It does not survive a map that is named, banded, and prints the places you marked. The galaxy exists nowhere else in the app; your held worlds are on Colony and on Fleets. The tab is called Galaxy and should open on it.

Three frames, one geometry. The map is 531dp tall at both 393 and 320, so it is the same drawing on a phone and in a Slide Over pane and there is one measurement to keep. The header loses the search field; the strip is gone; nothing scrolls.

The fold, and the one lie it tells
Two stars stacked vertically across a band gap are 25 systems apart, drawn 22dp apart. Horizontally, 25 systems is 337dp. That is a 15× understatement in one direction only, and it is the price of folding — a straight 250-star line at true pitch is 3,500dp and does not fit on anything. Three things pay it down: the turn is drawn, so the eye is given the path rather than left to infer a grid; the labels alternate sides, which states the reading direction without an arrow; and vertical neighbours are always in different named regions, which is the strongest "not next door" signal the map has. If it still reads as a grid on a device, the next lever is band gap, not layout.
Where the hour marks went
The reach strip's ruler is SurveyBalance: 30 minutes plus one a system, so the hours land at Δ30, Δ90, Δ150, Δ210. On an index-monotone path those are four hairlines on the map, and the strip stops being a component. They also say something the strip never did: home at 165 has three marks to its left and one to its right. Everything past you is inside 1h 55m; the far side of the galaxy is 3h 14m. You live near the edge, and the map tells you so without a word.
2× · Bramuvell Reach, Nerimira Deep, Kragith Blaze · the bias is the texture
Three adjacent bands, untouched, at twice size. Reach is an even field of standards; Deep is dark with one bright in it; Blaze is more than half bright. Nobody has to be told what a Deep is — the name and the texture arrive together, ten times, on one screen. This is the whole identity argument and it costs 55 µs.
393dp · day one · 0 surveyed · the default case
98% unsurveyed is the state the map is in nearly always, so it is the frame that had to be good first. It is also the frame the old ledger was worst at: five rows and a header. Here, on day one, you can already see that you are in a dark region, that the two brightest places in the galaxy are three bands up and three bands down, and how far each is.
2 · What the map knows about you
Four overlays and no more, each on its own channel, none of them leaking anything a survey has not paid for. A glyph may say how bright a star is, which region it is in and whether you have been there. It may never say what is orbiting it.

Colour obeys the portrait call: no status hue ever lands on a celestial body — heat is brightness, the cold end leans the deuterium violet, a third of the brights carry a crystal-leaning halo, and each band sits in a 5–10% tinted field of its temperament. Halos and fields are radial gradients, legal here because a star is the one thing the system lets glow. Size and luminance are class, so knowledge cannot be confused with astronomy: a surveyed dim star and an unsurveyed bright one are never the same mark. Amber is the fleet strip's amber and means the same thing it means there. Accent is the selection, and the selection is what the caption acts on, which keeps accent meaning "go tap this".
A pin becomes a label, and that is the whole of search
You cannot print 250 names — 144 µs says the drawing could, and the screen says it cannot. You can print the four or five you have marked. So on the map a pin does not sort anything: a pin is what makes a name appear. It is still exactly the state 0.11.0 stored — a list of world coordinates — and a system is labelled when it holds a pinned world, which is a derivation and not a new field. This is why the search box is not missed on the map. Search asks "where is the place I named"; the map has already written the answer next to the star.
Aiming, and the line the map will not cross
A probe is aimed at a star, so the caption's ghost aims one: probe 1h 00m, opening the dispatch sheet already pointed. A run is aimed at a world, and worlds are what a survey pays for, so the map cannot aim one and must not pretend to. On a system you already know, the ghost is replaced by the round trip in plain text and the caption pushes the orbit page, where the run has always been dispatched from. Stars are probe targets; worlds are run targets. One rule, and it falls straight out of the knowledge tiers.
The caption is never empty
The map opens with home selected, and a tap can only move the selection, never clear it. There is no "nothing selected" state to design, no placeholder copy, and no empty 58dp bar at the bottom of the screen. It also means the first thing the tab ever shows a new player is their own star, named, with its own clock on it.

What four discs can mean today
cost, honestlyThe four are not equidistant and that is the only real content the view has: G2 and G4 are one hop, G1 is two. A probe reaches a neighbour in 4h 40m and G1 in 8h 50m; a run is 9h 20m and 18h 20m out and back. Crossing your own galaxy end to end is 3h 22m, so a hop is 2.8× the longest journey you can make at home.
texture, freeEach disc is the same ten bands at 0.4 scale, drawn from that galaxy's own temperament permutation. It is real, not decorative — G1 genuinely has its Blaze regions where the picture puts them — and it costs four times 55 µs.
what it leavesThree empires arrive as three tinted discs and a holdings count on the line that reads 0 surveyed today. Multiplayer arrives as the same line with a population. Neither needs a new surface, and neither is drawn here, because a frame that shows an empire you cannot meet is the thing this brief calls identity-only.
not a screenIt has no back button because it is not pushed. The chip in the header is a toggle between two states of the map, the way worlds · map is a toggle between two states of the tab. Two toggles, no stack, and the tab bar never changes under you.
The ledger, itemised
filter chipsGone. They filtered a list that is fourteen rows long on a day-21 save. A control that narrows fourteen to nine is work with no reward, and every axis they filtered on — distance, verdict, region — is now a thing you can see rather than a thing you request.
the sortGone. A sort ranks on one axis, and "where next" is distance against class against region against what is still unknown. The map holds all four at once, which is the one thing a drawing does that an ordering cannot.
searchStays, on the worlds list only. It matches names you have learned, which is the definition of a list job. It was useless on the old landing screen for a reason that was never about search: you were asked to type the name of a place you had not been to yet.
pinnedStays, and gains a second job. A pinned world still sorts to the top here; the system that holds it is now the one the map writes a name against. One state, two surfaces, no new field.
the countStays, loses its trailing sort control, and the header's second row is now just 14 worlds. The map's own header carries the same idiom one scale up: 250 systems · 11 surveyed · 3 pinned.
4 · Four layouts I did not take
All four are index-honest except the first, which is the one you already declined. Red marks the defect.
free 2-D scatter
Declined on 2026-08-15 and the drawing shows why without the balance argument: two stars a thumb apart, 155 systems and 2h 35m between them. It is the only layout here that would need the metric to move.
archimedean spiral
Index-honest and the best-looking of the four. It fails on the one thing the second dimension was bought for: a region is a curved arc with no straight run to set a 100dp name on, and the inner turns are 40dp long at 393. It also puts the densest ink where the screen is narrowest.
ten cells in a ring
Ten named cells, plenty of room for the labels, and it closes — which is the defect. System 250 sits against system 1, four hours and thirty-nine minutes apart, at the one place on the drawing where the eye insists they are neighbours. A galaxy with two ends should be drawn with two ends.
the strip we ship
Perfectly honest, and it is drawn here to be measured rather than mocked: it is the same 250 stars with the same class bias, in one dimension. A region is 33dp; its name is 100dp. Nothing else about it is wrong, which is why the fold keeps everything it does and adds a second axis.
5 · Premises that did not survive the screen
the strip is an hour rulerThere are two clocks in the tab and nothing labels either. The strip's marks are SurveyBalance — 30m plus one a system — and the system header's "20m out and back" is FleetBalance, which is a different function of the same Δ and disagrees with it by about 6× near home. Today they sit two components apart on one screen. On the map they would be two rulers over one drawing, which is not survivable, so the map's marks are the probe's and the caption prints the run's figure beside the name. Worth deciding whether that is the right way round.
regions bias the starsThey do in GalaxyBalance, but RegionStrip.dc.html — the thing that drew them at 0.11.0 — shipped with a different table: 60/25/15, 34/46/20, 15/25/60 against the committed 60/30/10, 20/60/20, 10/30/60. The mock understated a Reach by 14 points of standard and overstated a Deep's brights by half. The frames here use the committed mix, which is why the three textures read further apart than 0.11.0 suggested they would.
the region index is a level0.11.0's measurement was right and its conclusion has expired. Ten names do not fit on 393dp of one dimension; they fit trivially on ten bands. So the chooser that measurement justified has no job left — the map is the region index, and a screen of ten rows behind a header tap is a tap the player pays for nothing.
the starfield sits behindEvery Galaxy screen draws Starfield under the content column. Under a map made of stars it is noise that cannot be told from data, and at 320dp it reads as extra dim systems. The map frames drop it; the worlds list keeps it. Decorative stars and real ones cannot share a screen.
250 dots need a pinchMine, and it did not survive the frame. A pinch buys names for unpinned systems and nothing else, because class, region and position are already legible at 14dp pitch. The caption gives you those names one at a time under your thumb, for free, without a camera, a pan, or a zoom state to restore on foreground. No zoom is a feature of a five-minute check-in. Reverse it if a device session says otherwise; the geometry does not change, only the camera.

7 · What it should feel like
Opening the tab should feel like unfolding a chart, not opening a database — and the test is whether a player who has never read a word of this can say four things out loud after five seconds on the genesis frame. I live in a dark region. The bright places are three bands up and three bands down. I am near one end. That one is an hour away. If the next round hears any of those in Davide's own words unprompted, the fold worked. If instead the report is that it looks like a spreadsheet of dots, the fold failed and the answer is a bigger band gap before it is a different shape.
The check-in test is separate and stricter: finding a place to send the next probe should take one drag and one tap, and the map should be a worse place to loiter than the ledger ever was. Nothing on it rewards being looked at twice.
